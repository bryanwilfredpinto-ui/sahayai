## PHASE 1 — PROBLEM VALIDATION

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exact problem | Indians who want to learn digital/AI skills or upskill don't know **what** to learn, in **what order**, can't **afford** paid courses, can't navigate **English-only** platforms, and are often **excluded** (blind/deaf/illiterate/rural). They start in the wrong place, waste months/money, or never start. |
| 2 | Who experiences it | Students, jobseekers, career-switchers, rural youth, homemakers re-entering work, PwD users, vernacular speakers, low-income learners. |
| 3 | How frequently | Career/skill anxiety is ~weekly for jobseekers & students; learning is a months-long sustained need. |

| 6 | Why they fail | Paywalls; English-first; **assume you already know what to learn**; engagement/sales-driven; overpromise jobs; **accessibility is an afterthought — only ~34% of EdTech tools meet WCAG 2.1 AA** ([SkynetIndia](https://www.skynetindia.info/blog/digital-accessibility-in-indian-edtech-platforms)); thin vernacular for Tier-2/3 ([EdTechReview](https://www.edtechreview.in/trends-insights/insights/indian-edtech-open-for-vernacular-education-play/)). |
| 7 | Top-10 life problem? | For a jobseeker/student/career-switcher: **yes** (income + education). For the general population: **no** — it is important but not daily-universal. |
| 8 | Weekly attention? | For active learners/jobseekers **yes**; for a one-shot roadmap, retention is a real risk (addressed in Phase 4). |
| 9 | Does it improve a core axis? | **Education ✅ · Income ✅ · Accessibility ✅** (and Govt-access via NPTEL/Skill-India surfacing). |

**Phase 1 verdict: PASS** — real, painful, important problem on three core axes. Proceed.

### What can Chitti do that NOBODY else does?
1. **Accessibility-first, not afterthought** — voice-first, ISL, 26 languages, blind/deaf/illiterate/rural from the start (vs the industry's ~34% WCAG-AA baseline).
2. **Free-FIRST surfacing** — ranks Govt-of-India (NPTEL/SWAYAM/Skill India) + corporate-free **above** paid, and tells you *why* if a paid course is the only option. No incumbent's incentive allows this.
3. **Analogy teaching in the user's world** (cricket / farming / share-market) with a research-backed "where it breaks down" caveat — pedagogy no platform ships.
4. **Honesty as a feature** — never overpromises jobs, never sits exams for you, points to *others'* free courses even when it earns nothing. The opposite of BYJU'S-era sales pressure.
5. **Routed through one dost (Vaani)** — the user never has to find/learn a platform; they ask in their language.

## PHASE 4 — DEVIL'S ADVOCATE (investor trying to kill it)

1. **Why it fails:** the market is consolidating into giants (Coursera+Udemy $2.5B, BYJU'S/Unacademy/PW). A free aggregator with no owned content has **no content moat** and **no revenue model**.
2. **Why users ignore it:** a roadmap is a **one-shot** artifact — weak retention; people forget after building it once. ChatGPT can spit out a roadmap for free in one prompt.
3. **Why it's unnecessary:** govt already offers free courses (NPTEL/SWAYAM); roadmap.sh already gives free DAGs; ChatGPT already tutors.
4. **Why competitors beat it:** they have funding, brand, placements, thousands of hours of owned content, and distribution. Chitti has none of those as a standalone.
5. **Why NOT build it (standalone):** as "another learning app," it loses. Don't build it to fight Coursera/BYJU'S head-on.

**Can these be overcome?** Only by **NOT being a standalone learning app.** They are overcome *if and only if* the product is scoped as the **accessibility-first, free-first, vernacular learning CAPABILITY routed through Vaani** — because then: distribution = Vaani's existing users (not a cold edtech launch); moat = the accessibility substrate + 26-lang + Golden-Rule honesty (which incumbents structurally won't replicate, it's against their paid/engagement incentives); content = *aggregated free + govt*, which is a feature (free-first) not a weakness; retention = Vaani brings the user back for everything, not just a roadmap.

### Verdict (honest)
- **As a STANDALONE learning platform competing with Coursera / BYJU'S: 75/100 → BELOW 80 → STOP.** Do not build it to fight the giants head-on; it would lose on content, funding and distribution.
- **As an accessibility-first, free-first, vernacular learning CAPABILITY routed through Vaani (per the locked Vaani-sole-interface decision): 82/100 → BUILD — but with strict scope and the weak dimensions addressed first.**

### Conditions before CEOS (the weak dimensions must have a plan)
1. **Moat:** lean entirely on the accessibility substrate + 26-lang + community voices + swarm — the things incumbents won't copy. Do **not** try to out-content Coursera.
2. **Retention:** it cannot be a one-shot roadmap. It must live inside Vaani (ask anytime, in any language) + tie to progress/reminders — otherwise retention kills it.
3. **Revenue/sustainability:** free-first means **no user paywall**; sustainability must come from the platform (free tiers + community voices), stated honestly, not from upselling learners.
4. **Honesty enforced in code** (already true): no job-overpromise, no exam-sitting, free-first ranking — these ARE the trust moat.

### Why Chitti should exist (one line)
Because **every incumbent is paywalled, English-first and engagement-driven, leaving the blind / deaf / illiterate / rural / low-income / vernacular Indian — the majority — structurally excluded**; an accessibility-first, free-first, honest learning guide routed through their one dost is the gap nobody's incentive will fill.

## CORRECTED POSITIONING & RE-SCORE (Sire, 2026-06-09)

**The lesson: define the category BEFORE scoring.** My first pass evaluated this
as a *learning platform / LMS* (→ 75, STOP). That was the wrong category. Sire's
correction: it is a **PROFESSIONAL INTELLIGENCE PLATFORM** — and a standalone
product AND a Vaani capability can both be true at once (cf. Google Maps,
ChatGPT, YouTube).

- **Primary:** profession-specific AI news (a CA gets GST + AI-accounting; a
  doctor gets clinical-AI; a farmer gets agri-AI).
- **Secondary:** explain the IMPACT on the user's profession.
- **Tertiary:** recommend tools · certs · prompts · learning (the cnai_* engines).
- **Competes with:** AI newsletters, professional research portals, career-
  intelligence platforms, AI-adoption advisors — **NOT** Coursera/Udemy/BYJU'S/LMS.

### Real research on the CORRECT competitive set (sourced)
- **AI newsletters:** The Rundown AI (2M+ subs, 50% open), Superhuman AI (1.5M+),
  TLDR AI (1.25M+), Ben's Bites (166K), Import AI. Subs grew **340% (2023-25)**;
  market **$2.53B (2026) → $6.74B (2030)** ([Readless](https://www.readless.app/blog/best-ai-newsletters-to-subscribe), [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-ai-newsletters-2026)). **Gap:** on a big-news
  morning they overlap **~80% on the lead story** — generic, English, US-centric,
  paid/sponsored, NOT profession-specific, NOT accessible, NOT vernacular.
- **Professional-services AI adoption:** org usage **doubled to 40% in 2026**;
  lawyers — legal research 80%, doc review 74%; accountants — tax research 69%;
  50% of lawyers see AI as a threat to the profession ([Thomson Reuters 2026 AI in Professional Services](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/technology/ai-in-professional-services-report-2026/)). Professionals are anxious about **what AI means for THEIR job** — no generic newsletter answers that.
- **Career/talent intelligence:** $4.31B (2026) → $11.76B (2034), 13.2% CAGR
  ([IntelMarketResearch](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/ai-talent-intelligence-market-46902)) — but B2B/enterprise (Gloat, SeekOut, Retrain.ai), not the individual Indian professional.

### What nobody else does (under this positioning)
Profession-specific (vs 80%-overlap generic) · impact-on-YOUR-job translation
(vs raw news) · accessibility-first + 26-language (vs inbox-text email) · free
(vs paid/sponsored) · actionable tools/certs/prompts tertiary layer · routed
through one dost (Vaani) — ask in your language, no inbox.
