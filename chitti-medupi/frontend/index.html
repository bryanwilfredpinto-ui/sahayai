📚 Why Jan Aushadhi works LIVE
Plain-English explanation for users who've never heard of generics.

Jan Aushadhi (literally "people's medicine") is a Government of India scheme launched in 2008. The medicines sold there are generics — same active ingredient, same strength, same dosage form, same CDSCO quality standard as the branded versions sold at regular pharmacies.

The price difference comes from marketing + brand cost, not quality. A ₹120 paracetamol box at a pharmacy can be ₹15 at Jan Aushadhi.

Chitti MedUPI's job: every time you scan a branded medicine, surface the Jan Aushadhi equivalent + the nearest store in one tap.

🎓 Learn — medicine literacy LIVE skeleton
Plain-English (and Hindi) primers on what generics are, what NPPA does, what schedule H means, what to ask your doctor.
🟢 What is a generic?
A medicine with the same active ingredient as a branded version, made after the patent expires. Same CDSCO quality, far cheaper.
🟢 What is composition?
The active ingredient (salt) + strength + form. e.g. Paracetamol 650 mg Tablet. Two medicines with same composition are equivalent for that condition.
🟢 What is NPPA?
National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority. Sets ceiling prices for essential medicines. Anyone selling above ceiling is breaking the law.
🟢 Schedule H
Prescription-only medicines. You cannot legally buy these without a doctor's prescription. Chitti always reminds you.
🟢 Bioequivalence
Two medicines work the same way in your body if they have same composition + bioequivalence (≥80%). All Indian generics meet this standard.
🟢 5 questions to ask your doctor
"Can I switch to a generic?" "Is there a Jan Aushadhi version?" "What's the cheaper alternative?" "How long do I take this?" "What if I miss a dose?"
