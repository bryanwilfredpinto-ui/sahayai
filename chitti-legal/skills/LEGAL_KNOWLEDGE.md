### 1.2 Schedules

### 1.3 Fundamental Rights — Chitti must explain in plain language
- **Art 14** equality before law / equal protection — `intelligible differentia + rational nexus`, expanded to **manifest arbitrariness** post Shayara Bano (2017) and Joseph Shine (2018).
- **Art 15** non-discrimination on religion/race/caste/sex/place of birth. 15(3) special for women/children; 15(4) for SC/ST/SEBC after First Amendment; 15(5) for educational institutions after 93rd Amendment; **15(6) EWS 10%** post 103rd Amendment (Janhit Abhiyan 2022 — upheld).
- **Art 16** equality in public employment. 16(4) reservations; 16(4A)/(4B) consequential reservation in promotions (M. Nagaraj 2006; Jarnail Singh 2018 — quantifiable data of inadequate representation required).
- **Art 17** abolition of untouchability. **Art 18** abolition of titles.
- **Art 19(1)(a)–(g)** freedoms — speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession (1(f) right to property deleted by 44th Amendment, now Art 300A).
- **Art 20** ex-post-facto / double jeopardy (autrefois convict) / self-incrimination.
- **Art 21** life & personal liberty — Maneka Gandhi (1978) made it the workhorse: includes privacy (Puttaswamy 2017), dignity, livelihood, speedy trial, clean environment, sleep, internet access (Anuradha Bhasin 2020), passport, marriage (Shafin Jahan), choice of partner (Shakti Vahini).
- **Art 21A** RTE (86th Amendment).
- **Art 22** safeguards in arrest & preventive detention.
- **Art 23 / 24** trafficking & child labour.
- **Art 25-28** religion. Test of "essential religious practice" (Sabarimala 2018 — pending review).
- **Art 29 / 30** cultural & educational rights of minorities; **TMA Pai (2002)** + **PA Inamdar (2005)** — autonomy with regulation framework.
- **Art 32** constitutional remedies — Ambedkar called it "the heart and soul." HC parallel under **Art 226** (wider — for any purpose including non-FR matters).

### 1.4 DPSP — non-justiciable but interpretive
- 39(a)(b)(c) — distribution of material resources for common good (Sanjeev Coke; State of Karnataka v. Ranganatha Reddy); 39A legal aid; 40 panchayats; 41 right to work; 42 humane work; 43 living wage; 44 UCC; 45 ECCE for under-6; 46 SC/ST welfare; 47 health & prohibition; 48 agriculture & cow protection; 48A environment; 49 monuments; 50 separation of judiciary from executive; 51 international peace.

### 1.5 Basic structure doctrine
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** 7:6 — Parliament may amend any provision except basic features.
- Identified features: supremacy of Constitution, secularism, federalism, separation of powers, judicial review, free & fair elections, rule of law, parliamentary democracy, judicial independence (NJAC 2015).
- Recent BSD applications: Minerva Mills 1980 (Art 31C balance), Indira Nehru Gandhi 1975, IR Coelho 2007 (Schedule IX), NJAC 2015, Madras Bar Association tribunal cases.

### 1.6 Emergency, federalism, S.R. Bommai
- **Art 352** national, **356** state (Bommai 1994 framework — JR allowed, floor test on legislature, secularism as BSD), **360** financial (never invoked).
- **GNCTD v. UoI (2018, 2023)** — Delhi services controversy and Article 239AA reading.

## 2. New criminal codes 2023 — BNS / BNSS / BSA (in force from 1-Jul-2024)

### 2.1 Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) — replaces IPC 1860
- 358 sections (IPC had 511). Reorganised chapters.
- Key changes:
  - **Section 103** murder (was IPC 302). 
  - **Section 101** culpable homicide not amounting to murder (was 304).
  - **Section 64–73** sexual offences (rape now Section 63; gang-rape 70; rape of women under 18 → death/life). Section 69 — **sexual intercourse on false promise of marriage** as standalone offence.
  - **Section 106** death by negligence — 5 years (medical professional 2 years); **hit-and-run** 106(2) — 10 years + fine (rule subject to political deferral).
  - **Section 109–113** terrorism / organised crime / petty organised crime / mob lynching brought into the general code — earlier under UAPA / MCOCA.
  - **Section 152** acts endangering sovereignty, unity & integrity — replaces 124A sedition; broader & narrower in patches.
  - **Section 304** snatching as a separate offence.
  - **Community service** introduced as punishment for petty offences (e.g., 226).
  - Adultery (377 IPC) — already struck down by Joseph Shine 2018; not re-introduced.

### 2.2 Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) — replaces CrPC 1973
- 531 sections.
- Highlights:
  - **Section 173** — registration of FIR (zero FIR explicit); 173(3) preliminary inquiry permitted for offences 3-7 years (Lalita Kumari framework codified).
  - **Section 187** — police custody — total 15 days but may now be spread within first **40 / 60 days** of total 60/90 day detention (departure from CrPC 167).
  - **Mandatory videography** of search & seizure (Section 105).
  - **Trial in absentia** — Section 356 — for proclaimed offenders absconding ≥ 90 days.
  - **Witness protection schemes** explicit.
  - Forensic visit compulsory for offences ≥ 7 years (Section 176(3)).
  - **Mandatory timelines** — charge sheet 60/90 days, judgment 30/45 days of arguments, charge framing 60 days from first hearing.
  - **e-FIR / e-summons / e-trial / e-evidence** scaffolding (Sections 530, 532).

### 2.3 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) — replaces IEA 1872
- 170 sections.
- **Electronic evidence parity** — Section 61: electronic record same legal effect as paper; **Section 63** elaborated certificate (successor to 65B IEA — clarifies the *Anvar P.V.* / *Arjun Panditrao* tension).
- "Document" includes electronic & digital records explicitly.
- Joint trial of electronic and physical evidence streamlined.

### 2.4 What Chitti must remind users
- IPC/CrPC/IEA continue to **apply to offences before 1-Jul-2024**. New codes apply to **offences on or after 1-Jul-2024**.
- Cause-list / case-number conventions in courts have changed but old cases proceed under old codes.

## 3. Civil & procedural laws Chitti supports

### 3.1 Civil Procedure Code 1908
- Order I parties · Order VI–VIII pleadings · Order IX ex-parte · Order X examination · Order XI discovery · Order XIV issues · Order XVIII evidence · Order XX judgment · Order XXI execution · Order XXII abatement · Order XXXIX temporary injunctions (with Section 75 + Section 89 ADR reference).
- Commercial Courts Act 2015 — > ₹3 lakh "specified value"; mandatory pre-institution mediation under Section 12A (Patil Automation 2022 — mandatory).

### 3.2 Limitation Act 1963
- 3 yrs contract (Article 55), 12 yrs immovable property recovery (Article 65), 30 days/90 days appeals depending on forum. Section 5 condonation (sufficient cause), Section 14 (proceedings in wrong court), Section 17 (fraud, mistake).

### 3.3 Specific Relief Act 1963 (post-2018 amendment)
- Specific performance now general rule (substantial performance shift). Liquidated damages — Section 14. Substituted performance — Section 20(4). Special courts for infrastructure contracts.

### 3.4 Indian Contract Act 1872
- Free consent (13-22), consideration (25), void agreements (24-30 — restraint of trade 27 with exceptions), 73-74 damages — **Kailash Nath Associates (2015)** — pre-estimate-of-genuine-damage requirement.
- Indemnity, guarantee, bailment, pledge, agency — sections 124-238.
- **Sale of Goods Act 1930** — implied conditions/warranties; sec 16 (merchantable/fitness); sec 27 nemo dat.
- **Indian Partnership Act 1932** + **LLP Act 2008** (governs LLP formation, designated partners, conversion).

### 3.5 Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996 (as amended 2015 / 2019 / 2021)
- Section 8 reference to arbitration; Section 11 appointment; Section 16 competence-competence; Section 34 setting aside (patent illegality only for domestic — Ssangyong, Renusagar lineage); Section 36 stay of enforcement; Section 37 appeal.
- Emergency arbitrator — Amazon v. Future Retail 2021 SC: enforceable through Section 17(1)+(2).
- Institutional vs ad-hoc; IBA Rules on Evidence and conflicts.

## 4. Family law — every personal-law system + secular framework

### 4.1 Hindu law (Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs)
- **Hindu Marriage Act 1955** — Sections 5 conditions; 13 divorce grounds (cruelty, desertion, conversion, mental disorder, virulent disease, renunciation, presumption of death, mutual consent 13B); restitution Section 9; nullity Section 11/12.
- **Hindu Succession Act 1956** — daughters as coparceners since **Vineeta Sharma (2020)** retrospective from inception of HSA, irrespective of father alive on 9-Sep-2005.
- **Hindu Minority & Guardianship Act 1956** — natural guardian father then mother (Githa Hariharan 1999 read down — mother independently natural guardian).
- **Hindu Adoptions & Maintenance Act 1956** — conditions for valid adoption, capacity of single woman to adopt.

### 4.2 Muslim law
- **Shariat Application Act 1937** for personal-law matters of Muslims.
- **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939** — grounds for woman.
- **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act 2019** — instant triple talaq criminalised (3 yrs imprisonment).
- **Shamim Ara (2002)**, **Shayara Bano (2017)**, **Joseph Shine (2018)** trio reshaped the doctrinal floor.
- Sunni/Shia distinctions — Hanafi vs Ithna Ashari rules on talaq, inheritance shares.

### 4.3 Christian law
- **Indian Christian Marriage Act 1872** — registration and forms of solemnisation.
- **Divorce Act 1869** (amended 2001) — Section 10 grounds parity post **Pragati Varghese v. Cyril George Varghese (1997 Bom)** + 2001 amendment.

### 4.4 Parsi law
- **Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act 1936** — Special matrimonial courts with delegates.

### 4.5 Secular / inter-religious framework
- **Special Marriage Act 1954** — 30-day notice & objection (constitutional challenge pending — privacy concerns); inter-faith marriage tool.
- **Foreign Marriage Act 1969** — Indian citizens abroad.
- **Indian Succession Act 1925** — applies to Christians, Parsis, and to Hindus in matters of will (Sections 57-191).
- **Guardians & Wards Act 1890** — secular guardianship.
- **Family Courts Act 1984** — exclusive jurisdiction for matrimonial matters where established.

### 4.6 Maintenance & welfare
- **Section 144 BNSS** (former Section 125 CrPC) — irreligious maintenance for wife/children/parents. **Shah Bano (1985)** → **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986** → **Danial Latifi (2001)** harmonious reading → **Mohd. Abdul Samad (2024 SC)** — Muslim divorced women entitled under 125 CrPC alongside the 1986 Act.
- **Maintenance & Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007** — Tribunal-based summary procedure; eviction of children misusing property remedy.

### 4.7 Children & women protection
- **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015** (am. 2021) — CWC / JJB; **Pratap Singh v. State of Jharkhand** — age determination test in **Section 94 JJ Act**.
- **POCSO 2012** — child = under 18, sexual offences, in-camera trial, special courts. **Independent Thought (2017)** — exception 2 to Section 375 IPC read down for 15-18 wives.
- **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006** — voidable + presumption.
- **Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005** — civil reliefs in criminal court; protection, residence, monetary, custody, compensation orders. **Hiral P. Harsora (2016)** — adult male limitation read down.
- **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act 2013** — IC mandatory ≥10 employees; LC at district; 90-day inquiry timeline; **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)** as the doctrinal seed.
- **Dowry Prohibition Act 1961** + BNS 80 (former IPC 304B dowry death) + 85/86 (former 498A cruelty) — **Arnesh Kumar (2014)** safeguards on automatic arrest reinforced via **Satender Kumar Antil (2022)**.

## 5. Property, consumer & specialised regulation

### 5.1 Property & tenancy
- **Transfer of Property Act 1882** — Sections 5-53A (sale, mortgage, lease, exchange, gift, actionable claims, doctrine of part performance 53A, lis pendens 52).
- **Registration Act 1908** — Section 17 mandatory registration list; Section 49 effect of non-registration.
- **Indian Stamp Act 1899** + state stamp acts — stamp duty as state subject.
- **Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act 1988** (am. 2016 / read down by **Ganpati Dealcom 2022 SC** — pre-2016 transactions cannot be prosecuted; 2016 amendments prospective).
- State tenancy laws — Maharashtra Rent Control Act 1999; Delhi Rent Control Act 1958; Tamil Nadu Regulation of Rights and Responsibilities of Landlords and Tenants Act 2017; etc. Chitti must scope state-wise.

### 5.2 RERA — Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016
- Registration of projects > 500 sqm / 8 apartments; 70% escrow for sale proceeds; **carpet area** definition; possession-delay refund + interest at SBI MCLR + 2% (model rule).
- Three-tier: RERA Authority → REAT (Appellate Tribunal) → HC under Article 226 / writ.
- Key SC: **M3M India / Newtech Promoters (2021)** — RERA prevails over Consumer Protection in respect of refund + interest, but consumer remedy not ousted; **Pioneer Urban Land v. Govindan (2019)** — homebuyers as financial creditors under IBC.

### 5.3 Consumer Protection Act 2019 (replaced 1986 Act)
- **CCPA** (Central Consumer Protection Authority) — new regulator with class-action and product-liability mandate.
- Pecuniary: District ≤ ₹50 lakh; State ₹50 lakh–₹2 crore; National > ₹2 crore (post-2021 rules raising thresholds).
- **Product liability** Chapter VI; **misleading advertisement** liability on endorser (knowledge-based defence).
- E-commerce Rules 2020 — fall-back liability of marketplace, mandatory grievance officer.
- **Mediation** mandatory pre-litigation step at first stage.

### 5.4 Information & data
- **IT Act 2000** — Sections 43A (compensation for negligence), 65 (tampering with source), 66 (computer-related offences), 66E (privacy violation), 67 (obscene material), 69 (interception), 70 (protected systems), 79 (intermediary safe harbour — read with IT Rules 2021).
- **IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2021** — Significant Social Media Intermediary obligations: traceability of first originator (struck down for WhatsApp by Bombay HC pending SC), grievance redressal, monthly compliance report.
- **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023** — applies to digital personal data; consent + legitimate use; rights to access/correct/erase/grievance; Data Protection Board; significant data fiduciaries; cross-border transfer notified-country approach. **Rules under DPDP** in draft 2025 — consent manager, child age-gating (under 18), retention purposes. Penalties: up to ₹250 crore per breach.
- **Information sharing for medical / financial / Aadhaar** governed by Aadhaar Act 2016 + DPDP overlay.

### 5.5 Labour codes (consolidation 2019/2020, not yet uniformly notified)
- **Code on Wages 2019** — minimum wages, payment timelines, bonus, equal remuneration.
- **Industrial Relations Code 2020** — standing orders 300+, trade unions, layoff/retrenchment/closure 300+.
- **OSH (Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions) Code 2020**.
- **Code on Social Security 2020** — gig & platform workers included.
- Until full notification, the legacy Acts continue (Factories Act 1948, ID Act 1947, etc.).

### 5.6 Environment & energy
- Environment (Protection) Act 1986; Air Act 1981; Water Act 1974; Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972; Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 (post-2023 amendment debate); NGT Act 2010. Public Trust Doctrine (M.C. Mehta).

### 5.7 Tax-adjacent (Chitti CA covers full depth — Legal handles overlap)
PMLA 2002 — predicate-offence linkage + ECIR; FEMA 1999 — civil-natured contraventions; Black Money Act 2015 — undisclosed foreign income/assets; Benami; Smugglers and Foreign Exchange Manipulators (Forfeiture of Property) Act 1976.

### 5.8 IPR — quick map
- Patents 1970 (post-TRIPS 2005 — product patents in pharma; Section 3(d) Novartis); Trade Marks 1999; Copyright 1957; Designs 2000; GI Act 1999; Plant Varieties & Farmers' Rights 2001; Semiconductor IC Layout 2000.

### 7.2 Doctrines & tests Chitti uses to reason
- **Proportionality test** (Modern Dental College; Puttaswamy Aadhaar) — legitimate aim, suitable, necessary (least restrictive), balance.
- **Manifest arbitrariness** (Shayara Bano) — supplemental to Wednesbury / classification test.
- **Doctrine of pith and substance** (Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee) — federal legislative competence.
- **Colourable legislation** (K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo).
- **Doctrine of severability** (R.M.D. Chamarbaugwalla).
- **Doctrine of waiver** of FRs — generally not allowed (Behram Khurshed).
- **Doctrine of legitimate expectation** (Navjyoti Coop Group Housing).
- **Public Trust Doctrine** (M.C. Mehta).
- **Polluter Pays / Precautionary Principle** (Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum).
- **Doctrine of eclipse** (Bhikaji Narain).
- **Constitutional morality** (Navtej, Sabarimala) — vs. social morality.
- **Sovereign vs Non-sovereign functions** for state tort liability (Kasturi Lal → N. Nagendra Rao).

### 7.3 Standards of review
- **Reasonableness** under Article 14 — Wednesbury → proportionality → manifest arbitrariness.
- **Strict scrutiny** — used sparingly in India (Anuj Garg; rejected in Saurabh Chaudhri).
- **Public interest litigation** — locus relaxation since SP Gupta, codified guidelines in Balco Employees Union (2002).

### 7.4 International law & domestic courts
- **Dualist tradition** — treaties not self-executing (Jolly George Verghese).
- **ICCPR / CEDAW / CRC** read as interpretive aids — Vishaka, NALSA (2014 third-gender), Pravasi Bhalai Sangathan.
- **NALSA v. UoI (2014)** — third gender, self-identification, horizontal reservation directions.

### 7.5 Judiciary & rule of law
- Collegium evolution — S.P. Gupta → S.C. Advocates-on-Record (Second Judges) → Third Judges 1998 → NJAC 2015 struck → Memorandum of Procedure under discussion.
- Tribunalisation — Madras Bar Association line (2010, 2014, 2020, 2021) — separation of powers + appointment safeguards.
- Contempt of Courts Act 1971 — Constitution Bench in Re Vijay Kurle (2020), Prashant Bhushan (2020).

## 8. Practical legal navigation (Chitti's most asked help)

### 8.1 Where to file what
- **Police FIR refusal** → SP under Section 173(4) BNSS → Magistrate complaint under Section 175(3) BNSS (former 156(3) CrPC) → High Court Article 226.
- **Cheque bounce — Section 138 NI Act** — Section 219 BNSS / NI Act demand notice within 30 days of cheque return memo, file within 30 days of expiry of 15-day notice. Pecuniary limit at JMFC.
- **Family disputes** — Family Court (where established) under Family Courts Act 1984; otherwise District Court Civil under HMA/SMA/Divorce Act.
- **Tenancy/eviction** — state rent control authority (where Act exists) or civil suit under TP Act + state-specific.
- **Consumer dispute** — district/state/national commission per pecuniary.
- **Service matter (govt employee)** — CAT (Central Administrative Tribunal) for centre; SAT for states.
- **Tax** — see [CA_KNOWLEDGE.md](../../chitti-ca/skills/CA_KNOWLEDGE.md) §1.7 / §2.10.

### 8.2 Time-bars Chitti must surface immediately
- Cheque bounce — 30 + 15 + 30 day clock.
- Workmen's Compensation — 2 years.
- Motor accident claim — no limitation since 1994 amendment, but file within 6 months for stronger reliefs.
- Industrial dispute reference — 3 years for discharge / dismissal.
- Section 34 Arbitration challenge — 3 months + 30 days condonation maximum.
- POSH complaint — 3 months + 3 months extension.

### 8.3 Legal aid & helplines
- **NALSA** — toll-free 15100; legal aid for women / SC-ST / disabled / industrial workers / undertrials / mass-disaster victims / mental disorder / persons in custody / annual income < ₹3 lakh (varies by state).
- **State Legal Services Authority** — Lok Adalats (Section 19/22 LSA Act), permanent Lok Adalats for public utilities.
- **Mediation** — Mediation Act 2023 (pre-litigation mediation now statutorily anchored).

## 11. Boundary — Chitti is not your advocate

This file makes Chitti **doctrinally literate**, not **professionally accountable**. Chitti does not:
- Draft binding contracts or pleadings ready for filing without a human advocate's review.
- Represent a user before any court / tribunal / authority.
- Issue legal opinions for transactions.
- Guarantee outcomes — every matter turns on facts and judicial discretion.

For any of the above the user must engage an advocate enrolled with the State Bar Council under the Advocates Act 1961. Chitti's job: make the user walk into that advocate's chamber with the right vocabulary, the right time-bar in mind, and the right remedy framed.
